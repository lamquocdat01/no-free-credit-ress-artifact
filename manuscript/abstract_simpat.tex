The credibility of simulation evidence is graded qualitatively or by uncertainty analysis, not in field events
replaced. For a camera perception system under a zero-failure acceptance test, a fixed count
of real events (\nNzero{} for 95\% confidence that at most 5\% are missed) must pass without a miss. We prove that,
without a sim-to-real link, independent simulation cannot shorten the test; that a larger
error probability buys an exact number of events; and that a synthetic twin paired with each real event
bounds the real miss rate by the twin miss rate plus discordance, which pays off only through a transfer bound
priced in calibration scenes. On \nMainScenes{} fixed-camera scenes, real misses are rare (\nPmainOPshort); at the
pre-specified operating point the twin as built replaces no field event for the median camera (\nTcamfinOPS{} of
\nMainScenes{} cameras save any), and a twin needing no real events of a new camera, if equally discordant (not built),
would save at most \nSavedOP{} events each (20\% target). The transfer bound stands at scene level but is withdrawn at site
level (\nSiteSm{} sites). Out-of-domain pass-through has two named modes: night and camera-jitter cameras are falsely
accepted (\nOODSNightfa{} of \nOODSNightcams{}; \nOODSJitfa{} of \nOODSJitcams{}, a real-shake scene).
